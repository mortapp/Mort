# MORT Real-World Problem Map

| Registry category | Real-world problem | Intended measurable outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teen earning and work experience | First-time workers lack trusted opportunity, history, and practical habits | More safe first jobs, repeat clients, references, and completed work histories |
| Adult and business hiring | Small local needs are too small for formal hiring and too risky for informal posts | Faster qualified responses, clearer expectations, fewer cancellations |
| Optional Guardian Mode | Families need bounded support without mandatory surveillance | Voluntary links, useful alerts, fewer privacy conflicts |
| Community utility | Schools, nonprofits, events, businesses, and neighbors have fragmented needs | More balanced local opportunity and repeat community partnerships |
| Discovery and matching | Noise hides appropriate jobs and opaque ranking undermines trust | Better qualified views and explainable matches after safety exclusions |
| Job lifecycle | Schedule and status ambiguity causes conflict and stranded work | Fewer stale jobs, clearer commitments, recoverable transitions |
| Applications and hiring | Applicants repeat effort and posters cannot compare fairly | Better response time, structured decisions, fewer silent outcomes |
| Messaging | Off-platform coordination and unclear state increase risk | Participant-only communication, accurate unread state, safer warnings |
| Trust and reputation | Ratings can be manipulated or stripped of provenance | Verified-job reviews, references, correction, explainable verification |
| Safety and abuse | Scams, prohibited work, grooming, harassment, and unsafe locations can emerge | Earlier prevention, immediate free controls, accountable human review |
| Financial experience | Users confuse preferences or estimates with processed money | Clear non-custodial records, education, dispute metadata, no escrow claim |
| Profiles and portfolios | Teens need credible history without exposing unnecessary identity/location | Private-by-default work evidence and public-safe sharing scopes |
| Healthy retention | Engagement mechanics can conflict with school, health, and work | Returns driven by useful opportunities and progress, with pause controls |
| Monetization | Revenue pressure can distort access or safety | Optional perks with free core access and SDK-authoritative entitlements |
| Accessibility | Marketplace work is blocked by inaccessible input, media, timing, or status | Equivalent task completion across text size, assistive tech, and input modes |
| Onboarding | Role, age, safety, and value are hard to understand up front | Faster first useful action with recoverable, optional education |
| Notifications | Important work changes compete with privacy and interruption | Category controls, quiet hours, accurate urgency, typed destinations |
| Marketing and growth | Local marketplaces fail when supply and demand grow unevenly | Measured neighborhood launches and credible partner acquisition |
| AI assistance | Manual work is costly, but opaque automation can harm users | Editable suggestions, explainable flags, human-owned decisions |
| Admin and support | Safety cases need restricted evidence and accountable ownership | Faster SLA-aware review, audit trails, appeals, and consistent resolution |
| Analytics | Teams need evidence without collecting a surveillance dataset | Privacy-preserving funnel, outcome, reliability, and safety measures |
| Privacy and compliance | Minor data, location, evidence, and consent require tight controls | Less collection, narrower access, transparent retention and rights workflows |
| Reliability | Mobile interruption and retries create false or duplicate state | Truthful recovery, idempotency, hosted-source reconciliation |
| Native iOS | Source parity does not prove device behavior | Verified permissions, lifecycle, accessibility, notifications, purchases, and release behavior |

Every metric needs an abuse guard and must avoid optimizing unsafe volume. Legal review determines whether a job is allowed; product metrics do not.
