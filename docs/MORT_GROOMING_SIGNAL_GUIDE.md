# MORT Grooming Signal Guide

Status: moderator training draft. Signals are contextual indicators, not proof of criminal conduct and not a basis for public labeling.

## Signal Groups

- Boundary testing: sexual jokes, invasive relationship questions, requests for secrecy, or escalating personal topics unrelated to the job.
- Isolation: changing a public meeting to an isolated place, excluding expected people, offering private transport, or discouraging check-ins.
- Off-platform migration: pressure to move to disappearing messages, private accounts, or contact methods that defeat MORT controls.
- Identity and age manipulation: pretending to be younger, using another person's account, asking the teen to conceal age, or person mismatch at arrival.
- Incentives and leverage: gifts, money, job promises, threats, blackmail, debt, housing, or transportation conditioned on secrecy or sexual conduct.
- Sexualization: requests for photos, body comments, explicit content, sexual services, or inappropriate touching.
- Information extraction: school schedule, exact home address, daily routine, private family information, credentials, or verification codes.
- Retaliation: threats to ratings, payment, employment, family, school, or reputation after a boundary or report.

## Context Rules

A single ambiguous phrase may be harmless. Review the job relationship, age roles, repetition, progression, power imbalance, attempts to bypass controls, prior reports, and immediate-danger indicators. Never infer guilt from protected traits, disability, communication style, neighborhood, income, or cultural difference.

## Triage

- Critical: active sexual solicitation, apparent exploitative imagery, trafficking/abduction concern, immediate meeting danger, direct threat, or extortion. Block transmission when supported, preserve relevant evidence, and escalate immediately.
- High: repeated secrecy/off-platform pressure, sexualized boundary testing, person mismatch, stalking, or escalating contact. Restrict contact and route to child-safety review.
- Moderate: ambiguous but repeated boundary issues or unsafe location changes. Warn, preserve limited context, and seek trained review.
- Low/informational: isolated nonsexual awkward wording without escalation. Offer safer rewrite and monitor only as policy allows.

## Reviewer Checklist

1. Confirm participant roles and job authorization without exposing school or residence data.
2. Read the minimum context needed; avoid unnecessary explicit-content exposure.
3. Separate scanner category from human finding.
4. Record facts, not diagnoses or criminal labels.
5. Consider the teen's safety plan, location stage, check-ins, blocks, and person-match result.
6. Apply interim controls proportionately and preserve appeal rights.
7. Escalate reporting/legal questions to authorized specialists.

## Model Limits

Keyword and pattern scanning cannot reliably determine intent, sarcasm, coded language, image content, or real-world danger. It may be evaded and may create false positives. Human review, reporting access, blocking, and real-world safety design remain essential.
