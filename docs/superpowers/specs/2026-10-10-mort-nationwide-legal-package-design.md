# MORT Nationwide Legal Package Design

**Date:** 2026-10-10

## Purpose

Replace MORT's current Indiana-centric / draft-positioned public legal package with a nationwide United States legal architecture intended for launch across all 50 states. The objective is not to claim that MORT is immune from lawsuits or regulation. The objective is to maximize enforceability, reduce avoidable contractual and privacy exposure, preserve mandatory nonwaivable rights, and make MORT's public legal package consistent with a teen-focused real-world marketplace.

The public site will stop displaying the phrase **"Draft — pending qualified legal review"**. It will instead display the effective date and ordinary legal-page status. The site must not falsely claim that outside counsel has approved the documents. Internal release metadata may continue to record that external legal review has not yet been completed.

## Scope

This design covers the public legal package generated by `scripts/build-public-legal-site.mjs` and served at `https://legal.mortapp.org/`.

Core documents:

1. `/terms/` — nationwide Terms of Service.
2. `/privacy/` — nationwide Privacy Policy.
3. `/dispute-resolution/` — Arbitration and Dispute Resolution Agreement.
4. `/us-state-law-addendum/` — state-law savings and state-specific supplements.
5. `/guardian-terms/` — parent / guardian consent and authorization terms.
6. `/verification-privacy-notice/` — school ID, school email, government ID, age assurance, liveness, and biometric notice.
7. `/paid-services/` — subscriptions, in-app purchases, billing, renewal, cancellation, refunds, and platform-provider terms.
8. Existing safety, child-safety, prohibited-jobs, payment-dispute, support, contact, accessibility, terms-of-use, and account-deletion pages remain but are synchronized to the new package.

## Design principles

### 1. Strong assent, not fake browsewrap

MORT must not rely on a sentence saying that merely visiting a webpage creates a contract. Contract language will state that assent occurs when a user receives conspicuous notice and then takes an action identified as acceptance, including an acceptance button, account creation after a linked notice, purchase confirmation after a linked notice, or continued use after conspicuous notice of a material update where that method is legally sufficient.

Purchases and subscriptions may reaffirm the current Terms and Arbitration Agreement **only when the checkout surface tells the user that completing the transaction constitutes agreement** and provides accessible links.

The public legal site should explain this architecture but should not claim acceptance events that the product UI did not actually collect.

MORT should retain versioned acceptance evidence: agreement version, agreement hash or immutable version identifier, account ID, timestamp, event type, guardian acceptance when applicable, and the acceptance surface.

### 2. Teen assent + guardian architecture

MORT's target marketplace population includes users ages 13–17. The contract must therefore distinguish:

- Teen assent to platform rules and account obligations.
- Separate guardian authorization where MORT requires it for specified marketplace, verification, payment, or safety functionality.
- Adult/business users accepting directly on their own behalf.

The Terms must not falsely state that a minor has waived rights that applicable law makes nonwaivable or that a guardian signature automatically cures every state-law issue involving a minor's contract.

### 3. Nationwide state-law savings structure

MORT will use one national Terms package plus a state-law addendum rather than fifty separate Terms documents. The addendum will state that mandatory state rights control over conflicting contractual language.

The addendum must be structured so that future state changes can be added without rewriting the full Terms.

### 4. Youth-employment law is a product eligibility boundary

The legal package must say clearly that publication, verification, acceptance, a badge, or a MORT safety classification is **not legal clearance for a job**.

Federal and state child-labor requirements can overlap. MORT will use the more protective applicable rule when federal and state law differ. Public language will reserve MORT's right to block or restrict jobs based on age, jurisdiction, school-day status, hours, hazardous occupation, permit/certificate requirements, or other legal eligibility.

The legal package must not promise that MORT's eligibility engine alone guarantees legal compliance.

### 5. High-protection teen privacy baseline

MORT adopts a nationwide teen baseline stronger than the minimum rules in many states:

- No sale of Teen User personal information for money.
- No cross-context behavioral advertising to Teen Users.
- Precise Teen User location is not an ordinary public field.
- School IDs, government IDs, school email, guardian data, safety reports, dispute evidence, and private communications are not ordinary advertising data.
- Sensitive verification data is retained for the shortest reasonably necessary period.
- Separate consent / notice is used where biometric, precise-location, or sensitive-data law requires it.
- Account deletion does not require deletion of records that must be preserved for unresolved payments, safety, abuse prevention, fraud, evidence, legal obligations, or similar lawful reasons.

### 6. Arbitration should survive scrutiny, not overreach

The Arbitration Agreement will cover disputes between a user and MORT to the maximum extent permitted by law. It will not purport to bind disputes solely between a Teen User and a job poster.

Required structure:

- conspicuous arbitration notice;
- 60-day good-faith informal dispute resolution;
- binding individual AAA consumer arbitration where enforceable;
- Federal Arbitration Act language where applicable;
- class / collective / consolidated / representative waiver where enforceable;
- jury-trial waiver where enforceable;
- small-claims exception;
- regulatory and government-reporting rights preserved;
- emergency / safety / child-exploitation / evidence-preservation / cybersecurity injunctive-relief exception;
- federal sexual-assault / sexual-harassment election preserved under 9 U.S.C. chapter 4;
- 30-day arbitration opt-out;
- state-law public-injunctive-relief savings language, including California;
- severability;
- survival;
- AAA fee allocation consistent with applicable consumer rules;
- no clause stating that a consumer can never sue under any circumstances.

MORT should use an electronic opt-out channel that actually exists. Until a dedicated legal address is configured, `mortapp.help@gmail.com` may be used with a defined subject such as `ARBITRATION OPT OUT` or `LEGAL NOTICE`. No fictitious physical mailing address may be published.

AAA consumer-clause registration is an operational follow-up before relying on AAA administration at scale.

### 7. Liability allocation

The Terms may use a contractual liability cap, but every cap must include a nonwaivable-rights savings clause.

Target language:

> To the maximum extent permitted by applicable law, MORT's aggregate liability for claims arising from the Services will not exceed the greater of $100 or the amount the claimant paid directly to MORT during the 12 months preceding the event giving rise to the claim.

The limitation must not purport to exclude liability where applicable law prohibits exclusion or limitation, including where required for fraud, intentional misconduct, gross negligence, death or personal injury, statutory remedies, or other nonwaivable obligations.

Adults and businesses may receive a third-party-claim indemnification obligation for their unlawful listings, wage / youth-employment violations, undisclosed hazards, infringement, fraud, intentional misconduct, misuse of personal information, or breach of the Terms. Teen Users will not receive the same broad indemnity obligation.

### 8. Marketplace role

MORT is a technology marketplace and safety platform unless law determines otherwise based on actual facts. The Terms may state that MORT does not become an employer, employee, staffing agency, contractor, insurer, fiduciary, guardian, transportation provider, or legal representative merely because users interact through MORT.

The clause must include a savings sentence that contractual labels do not override a classification imposed by applicable law.

### 9. Verification is not a guarantee

Verification may include school ID, school email, government ID, age assurance, business verification, liveness, fraud signals, and provider reference IDs.

A verification badge must be defined as proof only that specified steps were completed. It is not a guarantee of identity, criminal history, trustworthiness, job legality, financial responsibility, safety, or future conduct.

### 10. Paid services and automatic renewal

Paid Services terms must disclose price, billing interval, renewal, trial conversion, cancellation, and refund rules before purchase.

MORT will use a nationwide strict-standard design rather than weaker state-specific checkout flows:

- express affirmative consent to recurring billing;
- clear renewal terms before purchase;
- post-purchase acknowledgment;
- easy online cancellation for online enrollment;
- no artificial friction intended to obstruct cancellation;
- required renewal / trial / price-change reminders where applicable;
- platform-provider terms for Apple, Google, RevenueCat, Stripe, or other processors where applicable.

The legal package must not state that the vacated 2024 FTC "click-to-cancel" rule is currently binding federal law. State rules and ordinary FTC deception / unfairness authority remain relevant.

### 11. Security breach and privacy-rights language

Because every state has a security-breach notification statute and notice details differ, the Privacy Policy will use jurisdiction-sensitive language rather than one universal deadline.

MORT will state that affected people, regulators, consumer-reporting agencies, or others will be notified as required by applicable law.

Privacy rights language will support, where applicable, access, confirmation, correction, deletion, portability, opt-out, consent withdrawal, appeal, sale / sharing / targeted-advertising restrictions, sensitive-data limits, and authorized-agent mechanisms.

### 12. Public status presentation

Remove all visible occurrences of:

- `Draft — pending qualified legal review`
- `Draft`

from the public header, footer, index cards, and public-facing policy text when they are used only as status labels.

Replace with ordinary status such as:

- `Effective October 10, 2026`
- `MORT Legal & Safety`
- `United States`

Do **not** add `lawyer approved`, `attorney reviewed`, `legally certified`, or equivalent claims.

Internal metadata should remain truthful, for example:

```json
{
  "legalApprovalClaimed": false,
  "externalLegalReviewPending": true,
  "publicDeploymentClaimed": true
}
```

## 50-state compliance matrix

The public legal package must be capable of operating in all fifty states, but product eligibility may be narrower until operational controls support a state's requirements. The legal site does not itself authorize marketplace activation in a state.

### All 50 states — youth work

The U.S. Department of Labor's state child-labor tables confirm materially different hour, night-work, and certificate rules among states. MORT must treat the following as jurisdiction-sensitive for every state:

- minimum age for a specific occupation;
- school-day and school-week hours;
- non-school-day and non-school-week hours;
- night-work windows;
- hazardous occupations;
- employment / age certificate requirements;
- school authorization or parental permission where required;
- stricter federal rule when state law is less protective;
- stricter state rule when state law is more protective.

States in scope: Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming.

### All 50 states — breach notification

Every state has a breach-notification law. The legal package must therefore preserve state-specific notice timing, content, regulator notice, encryption exceptions, and other requirements rather than promising one uniform national procedure.

### Comprehensive privacy states / high-priority privacy supplements

The state-law addendum should specifically preserve comprehensive consumer privacy rights in states with enacted comprehensive regimes, including at minimum:

California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, and Virginia.

MORT should use the strict teen baseline nationwide, so states without an omnibus privacy statute still receive the same product-level teen protections unless law requires something stronger or different.

### Biometrics / facial data high-priority states

The verification notice must specifically account for jurisdictions with dedicated biometric rules. At minimum:

- **Illinois:** written notice, purpose / term disclosure, written release, public retention / destruction policy, no sale / profit from biometric data, and protective storage requirements where BIPA applies.
- **Texas:** pre-capture notice and consent for commercial biometric capture, plus statutory disclosure / protection restrictions where applicable.
- **Washington:** biometric-enrollment, disclosure, retention, and consumer-protection requirements where applicable.

The notice must also preserve rights under other states' broader privacy, health-data, consumer-protection, or biometric statutes.

### California supplement

The state addendum should expressly preserve, where applicable:

- CCPA / CPRA access, deletion, correction, sale / sharing opt-out, sensitive-information rights, and non-discrimination;
- required treatment of GPC when MORT is legally required to honor it;
- the under-16 sale / sharing opt-in rule, while MORT's own teen baseline is not to sell or cross-context behaviorally share Teen User data;
- California Automatic Renewal Law requirements for recurring services;
- public-injunctive-relief rights that cannot lawfully be waived in every forum.

### Colorado supplement

Preserve applicable Colorado Privacy Act rights and automatic-renewal requirements, including clear disclosures, acknowledgment, simple cancellation, and required notices.

### Vermont supplement

Preserve the affirmative opt-in and notice requirements that apply to covered long-term automatically renewing consumer contracts.

### Other states

The state-law addendum will contain a universal savings clause rather than pretending that a static legal page exhaustively lists every state consumer-protection, privacy, subscription, biometric, youth-work, wage, safety, or contract rule. If a mandatory state right conflicts with the Terms, the mandatory state right controls to the extent of the conflict.

## Under-13 boundary

Ordinary MORT accounts remain 13+.

MORT must not knowingly operate an ordinary account for a child under 13. If MORT learns an ordinary account belongs to a child under 13, the account may be restricted or deleted subject to narrow safety, abuse-prevention, evidence, fraud, payment, or legal-retention requirements.

The Privacy Policy must not imply that simply saying "13+" eliminates COPPA obligations. Neutral age screening and actual-knowledge handling still matter.

## Product-interface language that the legal package assumes

The legal package is strongest when MORT uses language substantially equivalent to:

**Account creation**

> By tapping Create Account, you agree to MORT's Terms of Service and Arbitration Agreement and acknowledge the Privacy Policy. The Arbitration Agreement includes binding individual arbitration and a class-action waiver, subject to stated exceptions and applicable law.

**Purchase / subscription**

> By tapping Buy, Subscribe, or Confirm Purchase, you agree to the applicable Paid Services Terms, reaffirm the MORT Terms of Service and Arbitration Agreement, and acknowledge the Privacy Policy.

The links must be visible and operable before the final action. Tiny, hidden, low-contrast, or detached notice is not acceptable.

## Known unresolved legal / operational issue

The precise legal contracting entity and legal mailing address remain unresolved. This design does not fabricate them. The public site may identify the service as MORT and use the existing support / privacy contact channels, but it must not claim a corporate status, registered office, or attorney approval that does not exist.

This unresolved point does not require displaying the word `Draft`; it does require avoiding false identity claims and is a priority item for eventual attorney review.
