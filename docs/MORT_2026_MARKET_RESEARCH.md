# MORT 2026 Market Research

Research refreshed: 2026-07-17

## Scope

This review looked at local service marketplaces, care marketplaces, freelance marketplaces, neighborhood products, youth-employment guidance, iOS interaction guidance, financial-habit products, creator/portfolio tools, and safety operations. The purpose is pattern research, not copying protected wording, visuals, code, or policies.

## Evidence-Backed Patterns

Taskrabbit describes identity/background screening, careful handling of sensitive information, support, evidence, and dispute processes. MORT should adapt the pattern as explicit verification status, minimum necessary evidence, role-limited access, and accountable support, without implying that an internal MORT check equals a regulated third-party screening. Source: [Taskrabbit trust and safety](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/46260491906203-Overview-of-Trust-and-Safety).

Rover emphasizes background checks, detailed profiles, verified reviews, on-platform messaging/photo sharing, support, and safe transaction handling. Its report/block guidance also puts the control directly in the conversation and warns against premature personal-information sharing. MORT should adapt protected participant communication, useful report reasons, block as an immediate user control, meet-and-greet preparation, and review provenance. Sources: [Rover safety](https://support.rover.com/hc/en-us/articles/205882216-What-does-Rover-do-to-support-safety) and [Rover report and block guidance](https://support.rover.com/hc/en-us/articles/206209920-What-if-someone-makes-me-feel-uncomfortable).

Upwork documents marketplace standards, graduated enforcement, appeals/support communication, identity verification, personal-data restrictions, and scam education. MORT should adapt clear prohibited-work rules, explainable restrictions, human review, limited evidence access, and education before punitive action when risk permits. MORT must not copy Upwork's adult freelance model because MORT's users include minors and local in-person work. Sources: [Upwork Trust and Safety](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/30236803116435--Upwork-Trust-Safety), [identity verification FAQ](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/34397755511955-Identity-verification-Frequently-asked-questions), and [personal-data protection](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/17995733233427--How-Upwork-protects-your-personal-data).

Nextdoor centers local alerts, recommendations, listings, events, groups, businesses, publishers, and public agencies. MORT's opportunity is to narrow this neighborhood utility to age-aware work and service outcomes: safe jobs, repeat helpers, event staffing, nonprofit/school partnerships, weather-aware work guidance, and public-safe opportunity links. Source: [About Nextdoor](https://about.nextdoor.com/).

Apple recommends fast and optional onboarding, contextual teaching, reasonable defaults, permission requests at the point of value, honest notification urgency, in-app notification controls, and accessibility from the start. MORT should use role-aware progressive onboarding, preserve state, request camera/photos/notifications contextually, never use time-sensitive notifications for marketing, and verify Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, focus, motion, contrast, and non-color status. Sources: [Apple onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding), [Apple notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications), [Apple accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility/), and [Apple inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion).

The U.S. Department of Labor explains that federal and state youth-work rules can both apply and the stricter rule governs; age, hours, school time, occupation, industry, and hazardous-work restrictions differ. MORT must treat jurisdiction logic as a reviewed policy input, not a one-time age check or legal guarantee. Sources: [DOL Fact Sheet 43](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/43-child-labor-non-agriculture) and [Youth Employment Compliance Assistance Toolkit](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/compliance-assistance/toolkits/youth-employment).

## Product Conclusions

1. Trust comes from explainable evidence, participant boundaries, responsive support, and fair correction, not a decorative badge.
2. Local utility improves when discovery connects to schedules, recurring needs, weather, schools, nonprofits, and repeat relationships.
3. Teen value is broader than one payment: work history, references, skills, professional habits, and safe repeat clients create durable value.
4. Adult value is reduced hiring friction with clearer job briefs, applicant review, scheduling, evidence, repeat-worker tools, and accountable closeout.
5. Healthy retention should follow real progress. MORT should avoid punishment streaks, infinite feeds, fake urgency, and pressure paywalls.
6. AI must remain assistive, explainable, server-mediated, editable, and reviewable. It must not infer sensitive traits or make final safety decisions.
7. Legal and operational capacity are product dependencies. Software alone cannot make a youth marketplace safe.

This research is product guidance, not legal advice or a claim that a competitor's controls are sufficient for MORT.
